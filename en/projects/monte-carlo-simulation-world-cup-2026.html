Project 15 · Football Analytics

Monte Carlo Simulation · 2026 World Cup Predictions

✅ publisheddata:2026

Simulation of 1,000,000 scenarios for the 2026 Football World Cup knockout bracket using Elo ratings. The analysis calculates the chances of reaching the semi-finals, playing in the final and winning the title from the probabilities of each tie.

CONTEXT AND OBJECTIVE

The project uses Elo ratings and Monte Carlo simulation to estimate national teams’ probabilities in the decisive stage of the 2026 Football World Cup. The analysis focuses on knockout ties and calculates the chances of reaching the semi-finals, playing in the final and winning the title.

The analysis uses ratings published by World Football Elo Ratings and the tournament bracket, including matches, dates, rounds and known results. Before modelling, teams, missing data, identifiers and connections between ties are checked.

This information is used to calculate each national team’s chances of advancing in its match and to simulate how the bracket could be decided. Winners of matches already played are retained as actual results, while remaining ties are resolved using probabilities estimated from Elo.

MODEL AND SIMULATION

Preparing the bracket and Elo ratings

Elo ratings compare national teams’ relative strength on a common scale. This project takes those values from an external source and incorporates them into each team’s data to analyse the difference in strength against its opponent in each match.

The knockout bracket is organised by round, identifying the connections between matches. This shows which teams already have a confirmed opponent and which could advance from earlier ties. Winners of matches already played are also recorded so they can be preserved during simulation.

For each match, the two national teams’ Elo ratings are compared. Their difference summarises one side’s relative advantage over the other: the higher a team’s rating relative to its opponent, the greater its chances of advancing according to the model.

ELO RATING DIFFERENCE
ΔElo = Elo A − Elo B

Elo A and Elo B are the ratings of the national teams playing the match. Their difference compares the teams’ relative strength before estimating their chances of advancing.

Probabilities of advancing in each tie

The Elo difference is transformed into a probability using the Elo system’s logistic function. This estimates each team’s chances of advancing and how evenly matched the tie appears.

PROBABILITY OF ADVANCING BASED ON ELO
P(A) = 1 / (1 + 10^(−ΔElo/400))
P(B) = 1 − P(A)

P(A advances) and P(B advances) are the probabilities of each national team winning the tie. They sum to 100% because only one team advances from each knockout match.

When both teams in a match are known, the probability is calculated directly from their ratings. For ties decided later, the simulation first resolves the earlier rounds and applies the same formula to the teams that advance.

Monte Carlo simulation of the knockout bracket

The simulation follows the bracket from the quarter-finals to the final. Each scenario preserves the winners of matches already played and uses Elo probabilities to simulate which national team advances in the remaining ties. Winners move to the next round until a possible tournament outcome is completed.

The process is repeated 1,000,000 times. Each simulation records the teams that reach the semi-finals, play in the final and win the title. The number of times each team reaches each stage is then counted.

PROBABILITY ESTIMATED BY SIMULATION
P(R) ≈ nR / N
N = 1.000.000

N is the total number of simulations and nR indicates how often a team reaches stage R or achieves outcome R. The relative frequency estimates its chances of being a semi-finalist, finalist or champion.

These frequencies are used to build a summary for each national team. Its chances of winning the title cannot exceed its chances of reaching the final, which cannot exceed its chances of reaching the semi-finals. Because the procedure is reproducible, probabilities can be recalculated when results are incorporated.

DELIVERABLES AND LEARNING

For each national team, the analysis obtains the chances of reaching the semi-finals, playing in the final and winning the title. It also calculates probabilities for confirmed ties and brings simulation results together in an ordered summary.

The charts compare the chances of winning the title and advancing through each stage, explore the most likely finals and identify the opponents each team could face along its route. The probabilities of those routes are calculated from how often they occur in the simulations.

The bracket structure connects match probabilities to the following rounds. The chances of reaching the final therefore depend both on each team’s strength and on the opponents it could face on its side of the bracket.

The project integrates data preparation and validation, comparison of Elo ratings, calculation of probabilities for each tie and Monte Carlo simulation. New results can be used to update the projections and analyse both the chances of advancing through each stage and the variability of routes across simulations.
