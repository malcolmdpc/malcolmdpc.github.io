PROJECT 21 · FOOTBALL ANALYTICS

Binomial Model · Goal Probabilities

✅ publisheddata:2026

A probabilistic model based on a binomial distribution to explore how goal probabilities vary with the number of shots and each club’s historical conversion rate. It estimates the most frequent outcomes and the probabilities of a home win, draw and away win. The analysis uses statistics from the 36 teams in the 2026/27 Champions League and includes a home-advantage factor.

CONTEXT AND OBJECTIVE

The project starts from the idea of modelling how many goals a team can score from its shots and the proportion that usually result in goals. With these two inputs, the binomial distribution calculates the probability of each combination of goals in a match and, consequently, the match outcome.

Using both teams’ probabilities, the analysis combines their possible goal totals to calculate the chances of a home win, draw and away win. This takes the reader from each club’s statistics to match-outcome percentages, showing how every value follows the same statistical approach.

DATA AND DIAGNOSIS

The statistics come from QueroStats, which publishes shot and goal records for clubs in the 2026/27 Champions League. For each of the 36 teams, the project uses matches played, total shots and goals scored, together with the name and identifier used to organise the dataset.

These records provide two parameters for each club: shots per match, obtained by dividing accumulated shots by matches played, and the conversion rate, calculated as goals divided by total shots. Both values enter the model’s dataset so that probabilities are calculated from each team’s statistics.

n\approx\frac{\text{Total shots}}{\text{Matches played}}
p=\frac{\text{Total goals}}{\text{Total shots}}

Shots per match are rounded to the nearest integer because the binomial distribution requires a defined number of trials, while the conversion rate is retained to two percentage decimal places to represent the observed proportion of shots resulting in goals.

Total shots include attempts on target, off target and blocked, so the conversion calculation uses the same set of attempts. This should not be confused with expected goals (xG), which describe chances differently by assigning probabilities based on their characteristics, such as the position from which the shot is taken.

Data preparation keeps both parameters separate: one represents shot volume and the other effectiveness, the two components that directly shape the goal distribution calculated by the model.

MODEL AND PROBABILITY CALCULATIONS

Binomial goal distribution

The binomial distribution describes how many successes can occur in a given number of trials. Here, each shot is a trial, scoring is a success and conversion expresses the probability of success. The model uses n for shots, p for probability per shot and G for goals.

G\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,p)

Shots are assumed to be independent and to have the same probability of resulting in a goal. This approach uses two easily interpreted parameters to obtain a complete distribution of possible goal totals.

Exact and cumulative probability

The probability of scoring exactly k goals accounts for every way those goals could be distributed among n shots. The formula combines the number of arrangements with the probability of converting the selected shots and failing to score with the remaining ones.

P(G=k)=C(n,k)\,p^k(1-p)^{n-k}
C(n,k)=\frac{n!}{k!\,(n-k)!}

The coefficient C(n, k) counts combinations of k goals from n shots. With 10 shots and a 10% conversion rate, the probability of scoring exactly one goal is 38.74%.

P(G=1)=10\cdot0.10\cdot0.90^9\approx38.74\%

To find the probability of at least k goals, we add the probabilities of reaching that total and every higher total. In the same example, the probability of scoring one or more goals is 65.13%.

P(G\ge k)=\sum_{r=k}^{n}P(G=r)
P(G\ge1)=1-P(G=0)=1-0.90^{10}\approx65.13\%

Both views appear in the line chart with a distribution for each team: EXACTLY shows the probability of each goal total and AT LEAST the cumulative probability from the selected value upwards. The 10+ category groups ten or more goals where appropriate.

Mean goals, dispersion and the Poisson alternative

The distribution provides expected goals, the theoretical average over many repetitions of the same conditions, and its variance, which measures how widely goal totals spread around that average.

\operatorname{E}(G)=n\,p
\operatorname{Var}(G)=n\,p(1-p)

Another common approach is the Poisson distribution, which can use shots multiplied by conversion as its expected mean λ. Poisson determines probabilities from that mean, while the binomial keeps shots and conversion separate. The latter lets us study each parameter’s effect, even when two scenarios have the same mean goals.

G_p\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda),\quad\lambda=n\,p

This is why the binomial model was chosen. Poisson and binomial probabilities can be very similar when shot volume is high and conversion low. As conversion increases, their different variances can produce distributions with different concentrations around the mean.

Joint goal probabilities

Each team’s distribution is combined to obtain the probability of every exact score. If L represents home goals and V away goals, the model assumes independence and multiplies the individual probabilities of scoring i and j goals respectively.

P(L=i,V=j)=P(L=i)\,P(V=j)

For example, a 2–1 probability is the home team’s probability of scoring twice multiplied by the away team’s probability of scoring once. The same procedure applies to the other possible score combinations.

The heatmap shows these joint probabilities in eight rows and eight columns, with home goals in rows and away goals in columns. The axes show 0 to 6 and the 7+ category, while colour intensity identifies the most probable combinations.

Probabilities of a home win, draw and away win

Goal combinations are grouped by comparing the teams: more home goals count towards a home win, equal totals towards a draw, and more away goals towards an away win.

P(\text{home win})=\sum_{i>j}P(L=i,V=j)
P(\text{draw})=\sum_{i=j}P(L=i,V=j)
P(\text{away win})=\sum_{i<j}P(L=i,V=j)

The RESULTS panel shows the three percentages obtained from these sums. Although the heatmap groups combinations into 64 cells, outcome calculations use both complete goal distributions and probabilities sum to 100% before display rounding.

Home-advantage factor

Home advantage uses shots by home and away teams in the league phase of the 2025/26 Champions League. The 2,213 home shots and 1,688 away shots give a ratio close to 1.311, equivalent to 31.1% more shots by teams playing at home.

M=\frac{\text{home shots}}{\text{away shots}}\approx1.311

This ratio produces complementary factors that increase home shots and reduce away shots while retaining both conversion rates. The adjustment affects shot volume and, through the binomial distribution, goal and match-outcome probabilities.

\text{Home factor}=\frac{2M}{1+M}
\text{Away factor}=\frac{2}{1+M}

When home advantage produces fractional shots, the distributions for the two adjacent integers are combined in proportion to the fractional part. For example, 12.3 effective shots use 70% of the 12-shot distribution and 30% of the 13-shot distribution, allowing probabilities to change gradually.

DELIVERY AND LEARNING

The project connects three levels: each team’s probability of scoring a given number of goals, the probabilities of home–away score combinations, and the final percentage chance of a win, draw or defeat. The charts make these levels and the origin of each result accessible.

The expected value summarises the average goals, while the complete distribution and variance explain how probabilities spread around that mean. Two combinations of shots and conversion can have the same mean but different probabilities of zero, one or several goals.

Home advantage adds a comparison by changing both shot totals and their distributions. The learning lies in applying simple statistical theory to football data and following, step by step, how calculations become home-win, draw and away-win probabilities.
