Project 19 · Football Analytics

Champions League 2026/27 Predictions

⚠️ in developmentdata:2026

The project estimates match and qualification probabilities in the league phase of the 2026/27 Champions League. It combines a Poisson goal model with 1,000,000 Monte Carlo simulations. The results bring together projections of points, positions and the chances of direct qualification for the round of 16, reaching the play-offs or elimination.

CONTEXT AND OBJECTIVE

The project analyses the league phase of the 2026/27 Champions League to estimate possible match outcomes and the final standings of the 36 teams. The work combines two stages: a statistical model that calculates expected goals and exact score probabilities, and a Monte Carlo simulation that projects how the competition could finish.

The working dataset was built from Champions League, Europa League and Conference League results from the 2021/22 to 2025/26 seasons. Records were prepared and validated to work with teams, goals scored and conceded, and home or away status. This information is used to estimate attacking and defensive strengths, together with the effect of playing at home.

The 2026/27 Champions League fixture list and incoming results complete the scenario for each update. Matches already played contribute their actual results, while the model calculates probabilities for the remaining fixtures. The simulations estimate the chances of finishing in positions 1–8, entering the play-offs from positions 9–24 or being eliminated in positions 25–36.

MODEL AND SIMULATION

Estimating attack, defence and home advantage

Goals scored and conceded in historical matches are used to estimate team strength. Goals scored provide information about attack; goals conceded, about defence. Each result is analysed alongside the opponent and home or away status, because the strengths of both teams contribute to the outcome of the same match.

Estimation uses Poisson regression. The model jointly fits attacking, defensive and home advantage coefficients to explain observed goals. More recent matches receive greater weight through time weighting. Regularisation is also applied to moderate extreme estimates and improve their stability, especially for teams with less historical data. Model fit and predictive ability were assessed using temporal validation.

These coefficients are used to calculate expected goals for each match. For the home side, its attack is combined with the away side’s defence, the overall scoring level and home advantage. For the away side, its attack is combined with the home side’s defence and the overall level. The exponential function transforms this combination into a positive expected goal count.

Expected goals per team
λL = exp(β₀ + βH + AL − DV)
λV = exp(β₀ + AV − DL)

Home λ and away λ express each team’s expected goals. A represents attacking strength, D defensive strength, β₀ the overall scoring level and βH the effect of playing at home.

With each results update, the model re-estimates the coefficients and calculates new goal expectations for the matches still to be played.

Calculating match probabilities

For each team, the Poisson distribution transforms expected goals into probabilities of scoring zero, one, two or more goals. The value of λ determines how probability is distributed across the possible goal counts. Each match therefore has a goal distribution for the home side and another for the away side.

Poisson distribution
P(X=k) = e^(-λ) λ^k / k!

X is the number of goals scored by a team, k is one of its possible values and λ represents its expected goals.

The two distributions are combined to calculate the probability of each exact score, such as 0–0, 1–0, 1–1 or 2–1. This calculation assumes independence between home and away goals, conditional on each side’s expected goals.

Probability of an exact score
P(H=h, A=a) = P(H=h) · P(A=a)

H and A denote home and away goals. The probability of the exact score h–a is obtained by multiplying the probability of the home side scoring h goals by that of the away side scoring a goals.

Exact score probabilities are then grouped by match outcome. The sum of scores in which the home side scores more goals gives the home win probability; scores with equal goals form the draw probability, and the remaining scores give the away win probability.

Home win, draw and away win
P(L) = sum P(h,a), h > a
P(E) = sum P(h,a), h = a
P(V) = sum P(h,a), h < a

L denotes a home win; E, a draw; V, an away win. Each probability sums the exact scores that meet that condition.

The same matrix identifies the most likely exact score and calculates the chance of over 2.5 goals or both teams scoring.

Monte Carlo simulation of the league phase

The league phase includes 36 teams and 144 matches, with eight matches per team. Each simulation preserves the actual results of matches already played. Exact scores for the remaining fixtures are generated from the Poisson distributions calculated for each match.

A standings table is built from actual and simulated results. Three points are awarded for a win and one for a draw, goals scored and conceded are added up, and goal difference is calculated. The final ranking uses an approximation of the sporting tie-breaking criteria included in the model, with random resolution when ties remain.

The procedure is repeated 1,000,000 times. Each simulation produces a complete standings table; together, the simulations form distributions of points and positions for all teams. Probabilities are estimated by counting how often each outcome of interest occurs.

Probability estimated by simulation
P(A) ≈ Nₐ / N
N = 1.000.000

N represents the total number of simulations and Nₐ indicates how many meet the event being analysed. Their ratio is the estimated probability.

For each team, the model counts how many simulations finish in positions 1–8, 9–24 or 25–36. These frequencies become the probabilities of direct qualification for the round of 16, reaching the play-offs and elimination. Expected points and possible position ranges are also obtained from the same simulations.

DELIVERABLES AND LEARNING

The project outputs include a summary for each team with its probabilities of finishing in the Top 8, reaching the play-offs or being eliminated. They also include expected points and positions, medians and percentiles showing how simulation results are distributed. Visualisations allow teams to be compared and the differences between their scenarios to be examined.

The analysis also presents match-level information: home and away expected goals, win, draw and loss probabilities, the most likely exact score, over 2.5 goals and both teams to score. For qualification, the distributions of points at the cut-off positions and the probabilities of reaching each zone given the points obtained are studied.

Each update incorporates recent actual results, re-estimates attacking and defensive strengths, calculates probabilities for the remaining matches and reruns Monte Carlo. Scenarios are identified by their cut-off date, allowing changes in qualification chances, expected points and projected positions to be compared.

The project integrates data preparation and validation, estimation of attacking and defensive strengths, calculation of exact score probabilities and simulation of the league phase. As new matches are incorporated, this process updates the projections and presents both the central expectation for points and positions and their variability across simulations.
